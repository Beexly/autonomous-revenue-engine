# Prompt — GLM 5.2, creative take on the Charcuterie Chick samples

Paste everything below the line.

---

You are a creative director reviewing work in progress. I want your own point of
view, not a checklist.

## The business

Charcuterie Chick, also trading as The Chick Goodies. Owner: Tricia Holfelder,
Tomball, Texas. Thirty-five years in restaurants, front of house and back of
house. Woman-owned, one party at a time, English and Spanish. She serves Tomball,
The Woodlands, Spring and Conroe.

What she actually sells: catering and events — weddings, receptions, showers,
parties, corporate. Grazing tables by the guest, carts and stations, sliders,
dips, salads, sweets, sips. Her own words, which you may quote: "elegance without
the cost", "Where Flavor Takes The Lead", "Houston's largest charcuterie cart",
scratch-made breads, jellies and jams.

The brief, in her husband's words and mine:

1. **Catering and events front and centre.** Weddings and events, not carts. The
   carts are one thing she does, not the thing she is. Every one of these pages
   has to say "we cater your event" before it says anything else.
2. **Elegant.** That word came from them, unprompted.
3. **Boutique.** Small, personal, hers — not a chain, not a template.
4. **Feminine, fun, engaging.** Elegant should not mean cold, and it should not
   mean plain.

Her customers are brides, mothers of brides, and event planners. Her own
audience is not technical. Neither is she.

## What I want from you

Read every page first. Then answer these five, in this order.

1. **Which direction is right for her, and why.** Pick one. Argue it in her
   customers' terms, not in design vocabulary. If the right answer is to take
   the bones of one and the skin of another, say exactly which parts.

2. **The one idea each site is missing.** Not a nit. The thing that would make a
   bride stop scrolling and text her.

3. **Where each site fails the brief.** Be specific: where does it still read as
   "charcuterie boards" instead of "catering and events"? Where does "elegant"
   slip into "plain"? Where does "boutique" read as "template"? Name the page and
   the section.

4. **Your own direction.** If you had this brief cold, what would you have built
   instead? Colour, type, motion, structure, and the first three seconds on a
   phone. One paragraph is enough if it is the right paragraph.

5. **Ten changes I could ship this week**, ranked by what they'd do for her
   bookings. Concrete enough to hand to a builder.

## Rules that cannot bend

- **Facts.** Every claim on these pages traces to a source document. Do not
  invent prices, review counts, ratings, years in business, awards, opening
  hours, response times or availability. If you want a claim on the page that
  isn't there, write it as "ask Tricia for X" — don't write the number.
- **Photography.** Her own photographs only. Never generated food, never stock.
  Cleaning, colour-correcting, upscaling or restoring her own files is fine.
- **Build.** Static HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework, no build step, no new
  dependency, no video. (`table.html` is the one WebGL exception.)
- **Nothing about the agency**, the proposal, or what any of this costs appears
  anywhere on her pages.
- **Never fix a visual problem by hiding the element.**
- Accessibility is not optional: text must hold AA contrast, and no page may
  scroll sideways at 390px.

## How to answer

Be specific and be opinionated. Name the page, the section, the selector when you
have it. I would rather have five sharp calls than twenty safe ones. If you think
a whole direction is dead, say so plainly and say what dies with it.
